# Överklagande av avslag på ansökan om svenskt medborgarskap

Appeal to a Swedish migration court against a citizenship refusal. Built for one profile: applied
under the pre-June 2026 law, met the old five-year hemvistkrav, refused under the new eight-year
requirement because the agency decided after 6 June 2026. Check scope first (§3).

This file gives the available arguments and where they come from. **You write the document**, in
Swedish, from this applicant's facts, in whatever order those facts make strongest. Two appeals
built from this file should not read alike.

---

## 1. Operating instructions

**Person appealing:** paste this file into a capable AI assistant, paste your decision (`beslut`),
ask for a draft. Answer its questions. You'll get a document with labelled blanks for your details.

**Assistant:** read it all first. Compute the deadline (§5) and lead with it. Ask the §3 questions
— only those, in English. Pick grounds and moves (§4, §9). Compose the document itself in original
Swedish prose. Close per §12.

- **Two languages, kept apart.** Talk to the user in **English** by default — questions,
  explanations, the list of remaining blanks, the closing handoff. If the user writes in another
  language, switch to theirs and stay there. The **filed document is always in Swedish**, whatever
  language the conversation is in; so are quotations from the decision and the authorities. **The
  one exception is the blanks: label them in English** — `[DATE OF REGISTRATION IN FOLKBOKFÖRINGEN]`,
  not `[DATUM FÖR FOLKBOKFÖRING]`. English labels are easier for the appellant to fill in, and they
  stand out sharply against Swedish text, so an unfilled one is hard to miss on a final read.
- **Never invent a fact about the applicant** — no dates, employers, income figures, or sentences
  attributed to the decision. Leave an English-labelled blank and list all of them at the end. A
  gap is fine; a fabrication is fatal.
- **Blanks are for data the user holds, and nothing else.** Dates, names, figures, document
  references. ⚠ Never leave a blank for the law, for an argument, or in a heading. "[OM DET KAN
  STYRKAS: den dåvarande lydelsen av] 11 §" treats a statute's prior wording as a matter of proof —
  it is not; look it up or state it plainly. A bracketed instruction telling the user to write the
  substance of a section themselves is the argument left unwritten, not a blank. If you lack the
  facts for a section, ask (§3, round two) or don't plead it.
- **Don't ask for personal identifiers — blank them.** Never prompt for personnummer, full name,
  address, telephone, e-mail or dossiernummer. They add nothing to the drafting and the user
  shouldn't have to put them in a chat window. Every one goes straight into the document as a blank
  the appellant fills in offline. The same applies to anything else identifying that the argument
  doesn't turn on: an employer's name, a child's name, a diagnosis. Ask for the *shape* of a fact —
  "continuously employed by the same employer since [date]" — and leave the identifying part blank.
  Ask for the minimum that makes the draft credible, and nothing more.
- **Never invent an authority.** Everything cited here is real and pinpointed. Add nothing from
  memory. If a point needs support this file lacks, drop the point or make it without a citation.

## 2. Ground rules

- **Plead only what is arguable on its merits.** Every ground in this file is included because it
  is worth making; none is here to occupy the court's time. If a ground doesn't fit the facts, drop
  it.
- Write what is provable; Migrationsverket will check. Concede what is true — an appeal that
  concedes nothing reads as one nobody checked.
- **Don't promise an outcome.** This fact pattern raises a contested question of law on which the
  legislative history is substantially against the appellant (bet. 2025/26:SfU28), and a carefully
  built appeal can still fail. Set expectations honestly and early, without discouraging someone
  from bringing a claim they are entitled to bring.
- **Plead each ground properly the first time.** An argument not raised at first instance is harder
  to run on any further appeal, so a ground worth including is worth developing — not gestured at
  as a placeholder.
- Not legal advice, and the output is not a finished appeal. Responsibility for what is filed
  rests with the appellant. Say so, and recommend a Swedish migration lawyer review the final text
  and that the appellant read up on how arguments and sources are presented in Swedish
  administrative proceedings before filing.
- **Stop if Säkerhetspolisen appears anywhere in the file** — those appeals go to the Government,
  not a migration court (27 § medborgarskapslagen), and none of this applies.

## 3. Diagnosis — two short rounds

**Scope gate, before anything else.** This file is built for one situation: an application filed
**before** 6 June 2026 and refused **on or after** that date, where the refusal turns on the new
hemvistkrav. Check both dates first.

- **Applied on or after 6 June 2026** → there is no intertemporal argument. The applicant applied
  under the law that was refused. Ground A is unavailable; say so plainly. Grounds B and C may
  still work if the exception question went unreasoned.
- **Decision issued before 6 June 2026** → the amendment isn't the cause of the refusal and none of
  this applies. Help them read their actual refusal ground instead.
- **Refusal rests on identitet, hederligt levnadssätt, försörjning or kunskapskrav** rather than
  residence → this file only partly fits; 11 c § st. 2–4 restrict exceptions for those. Say what
  transfers and what doesn't.

⚠ Don't stretch the file to cover a case it wasn't built for. Telling someone their situation
needs different arguments is more useful than a confident draft aimed at the wrong target.

**Scope gate — check before anything else.** This file is built for applications **filed before
6 June 2026 and decided after it**. If the decision issued before 6 June 2026, or the application
was filed after it, stop: the intertemporal problem this file is built around doesn't arise, most
of Ground A is inapplicable, and the user needs different arguments. Say so plainly rather than
adapting these grounds to a case they don't fit.

**Round one — three questions, always.** These select the grounds.

1. **What does the refusal rest on, and what does it say about exceptions?** Reasoning verbatim.
   Looking for: whether it rests on the eight-year hemvistkrav alone or also on identitet,
   hederligt levnadssätt, försörjning (11 b §) or kunskapskrav; whether 11 c § is disposed of by a
   bare unreasoned sentence; whether the remaining conditions are addressed at all.
2. **Was there a dröjsmålstalan, and did a court order the agency to decide?** Dates of the begäran
   (12 § FL), the refusal, any appeal, any föreläggande (49 § FL) — with målnummer.
3. **Is the dagboksblad in hand, and when is the deadline?** Date the decision was sent; whether a
   kontrollmeddelande followed.

Check without asking: whether **11 a §** gives this applicant a shorter period than eight years
(nordisk medborgare, statslös, flykting, married or cohabiting with a Swedish citizen). If it
applies and was overlooked, that plain error leads the appeal, ahead of everything here.

**Round two — only what the pleaded grounds need.** Once the grounds are fixed, ask for the facts those
moves run on. Short, specific, one round.

- **Ground B (always, if pleaded):** the *kinds* of tie that exist and roughly since when — work,
  family, housing, study, language, associations, elected or voluntary positions, civic or cultural
  participation — and for each, whether a document exists. Names and identifying particulars are
  not needed; they become blanks.
- **Ground A:** application date, arrival and folkbokföring dates, when the case was initiated and
  decided, any periods abroad.
- **Any ground:** the dates needed for the service computation, if not already given.

⚠ **Never plead Ground B without asking round two.** The samhörighet argument is made of the
applicant's facts and nothing else. A Ground B section that hands the user a bracketed instruction to
supply the substance themselves is not a draft — it is the argument left unwritten. If the user
declines or can't answer, say plainly that Ground B can't be pleaded on this record and drop it,
rather than pleading it empty.

⚠ Ask for facts, not identifiers. You need to know that continuous employment exists and when it
began, not who the employer is; that there is a child in Sweden, not the child's name. Never ask
for personnummer, address, telephone, e-mail or dossiernummer at all.

## 4. Grounds and remedy

Stacking. Everyone with an unreasoned refusal has Ground C. **Grounds are pleaded in the order
A, B, C** — the order they appear in below and in §9. Every ground ends in remand: the agency
usually hasn't examined the remaining conditions, and the court shouldn't be first to do so.

| Ground | Available when | Relief it seeks |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Application complete and decision-ready under the old law before 6 June 2026 | Set aside; remand for examination under 11 § as it read before 6 June 2026 |
| **B** | Real, evidenceable ties to Sweden | Declare särskilda skäl exist as to hemvistkravet; set aside; remand for the remaining conditions |
| **C** | Exception question disposed of without reasons | Set aside; remand for the 11 c § assessment never made, and for the remaining conditions |

- **A1 — a court föreläggande went unanswered.** Strongest pattern: the applicable law was
  determined by non-compliance with a court order, not by a queue.
- **A2 — delay without a föreläggande.** Viable but materially weaker; nearer the ordinary backlog
  the legislature knowingly declined to protect. ⚠ Don't dress A2 as A1. Worth pleading in
  between: a 12 § FL begäran made and refused but never reaching a court.

Tell the user where they sit. A Ground C appeal on its own is a proper appeal: where the exception
question was decided without reasons, the appellant is entitled to have it decided properly.

**Two things that shape the drafting.**

- **Keep the legal question clean and separable.** Any further appeal to Migrationsöverdomstolen
  requires prövningstillstånd, which turns largely on prejudikatintresse. So don't bury Ground A
  inside Ground C, and don't let a fact-bound särskilda-skäl argument swallow a point of principle.
  A clearly framed question is easier for a higher court to take up, and easier for this court to
  answer.
- **Swedish law is applied as it stands when a case is examined.** That cuts both ways here: it is
  why the amendment reached this application at all, and it is why the law applicable to a case
  still under examination is whatever is in force at that point. State it as the neutral principle
  it is — not as a prediction that the rules will change, and never as a reason to prolong
  proceedings.

**Yrkanden — keep close to this wording.** The court grants what is asked for, so this is the one
place imprecision costs the remedy rather than the argument.

> Klaganden yrkar i första hand att migrationsdomstolen, med upphävande av det överklagade
> beslutet, visar målet åter till Migrationsverket för prövning av ansökan enligt 11 §
> medborgarskapslagen i dess lydelse före den 6 juni 2026.

> I andra hand yrkar klaganden att migrationsdomstolen, med förklaring att särskilda skäl enligt
> 11 c § medborgarskapslagen föreligger såvitt avser hemvistkravet, upphäver det överklagade
> beslutet och visar målet åter till Migrationsverket för prövning av om övriga förutsättningar
> för naturalisation är uppfyllda.

> I tredje hand yrkar klaganden att migrationsdomstolen upphäver det överklagade beslutet och
> visar målet åter till Migrationsverket för den prövning enligt 11 c § medborgarskapslagen som
> inte har gjorts och för prövning av övriga förutsättningar för naturalisation.

Four rules, all of them hard:

- **Order by reach, not by confidence: A, then B, then C.** The court takes the yrkanden in order
  and stops at the first it grants, so pleading the narrowest relief first invites disposal on the
  narrowest ground — the stronger relief is never reached. Put the most far-reaching first *even
  though* it is the least likely to succeed; that is what "i andra hand" is for. Renumber to the
  grounds actually pleaded and drop the ordinal phrase if there is only one yrkande.
- **Never condition a yrkande on the court accepting your own ground.** "Om domstolen finner att
  den äldre lydelsen ska tillämpas, yrkar klaganden att…" concedes the ground in the act of
  pleading it. Ask for the relief outright; the argument for it belongs in section 4.
- **Every yrkande has an argument, every argument has a yrkande.** Map them against each other
  before finalising. ⚠ The common failure is a section 4 subsection that develops a ground — most
  often A — and then trails off into supporting some *other* ground's relief, asking the court for
  nothing on its own account. If a ground is worth a subsection it is worth a yrkande; if you
  won't ask for its relief, cut the subsection.
- **Never ask the court to grant citizenship outright** where the agency hasn't examined the
  remaining conditions. Remand is the appropriate relief: the assessment belongs to the agency in
  the first instance, and a court asked to make it afresh has no record prepared for the purpose.

## 5. Deadline and filing

- Three weeks from delfående (44 § FL).
- Förenklad delgivning: service deemed two weeks after sending, if a kontrollmeddelande went out
  the next day (22–26 §§ delgivningslagen [2010:1932]). Sent day 0 → delfående day 14 → deadline
  day 35.
- Show the arithmetic as the last paragraph of BAKGRUND; recompute it independently.
- File with **Migrationsverket, addressed to the court the decision names** (43 § FL; forum per
  26 § medborgarskapslagen). They forward it.
- ⚠ An incomplete appeal filed in time beats a perfect one filed late. Reserve the right to
  supplement, and use it.
- If the migration court dismisses the appeal, a further appeal lies to Migrationsöverdomstolen —
  three weeks from the judgment, and it requires prövningstillstånd. Worth telling the user, since
  that is where the law on this question realistically moves.

## 6. Dagboksblad

Evidentiary backbone of Ground A, corroboration for Ground C. Request by e-mail citing the
dossiernummer and partsinsyn under 10 § FL, asking for the dagboksblad and all aktbilagor
electronically. Free, usually days.

Look for: the aktbilaga registering any föreläggande; stretches with no recorded
handläggningsåtgärd, especially between the föreläggande and the decision and around 6 June 2026;
absence of any supplementation request, which shows the case was ready to decide.

If it hasn't arrived, file anyway on what's provable — that it's been requested and will follow,
that the föreläggande was made per the court's decision in the named mål, that no supplementation
was requested. ⚠ Never cite an aktbilaga number you haven't seen.

⚠ **The hard rule: don't describe a document you don't have.** Unless the user has confirmed the
dagboksblad is in hand, the phrases *av dagboksbladet framgår*, *av dagboksbladet framgår inte*,
and *dagboksbladet visar* are forbidden anywhere in the draft — including in BAKGRUND, in the
argument, and in the bevisning list. They assert the contents of a document the appellant cannot
produce, under paragraphs the whole ground rests on.

**Two states, and never both.** Either the diary is in hand — exhibit it, characterise it, cite the
aktbilaga — or it isn't, in which case say only that it has been requested and will be submitted on
receipt, and ask the court to obtain the file (7 § förvaltningsprocesslagen; the agency holds it
anyway). ⚠ A draft that characterises the diary's contents *and* asks the court to order its
production is internally contradictory and tells the court the assertion was invented. If you find
yourself writing both, you are in the second state: delete the characterisations.

Where the diary isn't available, Ground A still runs — on the application date, the entry into
force, the föreläggande and the decision date, all of which are provable from documents the
appellant has. Say what those dates show and stop there.

## 7. Document skeleton

Order fixed, content yours. Numbered paragraphs (3.1, 4.2.3 …), continuous prose, no bullets or
bold in the filed document.

- **Head** — court, plus a line that the appeal is filed with Migrationsverket per the decision's
  instructions; place and date; "ÖVERKLAGANDE"; klagande with personnummer and contact details;
  motpart; the decision appealed (date, dossiernummer); saken.
- **1 YRKANDEN** — graduated, per §4.
- **2 GRUNDER** — one or two sentences per ground, mirroring the yrkanden. Summary only.
  Optionally precede it with a short *Rättsfrågor* section naming the legal questions the court must resolve; useful discipline where more than one ground is pleaded.
- **3 BAKGRUND** — the factual record everything else stands on. See §8.
- **4 UTVECKLING AV TALAN** — one subsection per ground, same order as the yrkanden; rule with
  authorities first, then application to these facts.
- **5 BEVISNING** — numbered exhibits, each with a bevistema ("till styrkande av att …"). The
  appealed decision needs none. ⚠ **Be sparing.** Exhibit only what a pleaded ground actually turns
  on. Three things in particular do *not* need proving: anything already in Migrationsverket's own
  file, which the court can obtain from the agency (say so, and ask for it, instead of assembling
  it); the applicant's satisfaction of the pre-6 June requirements, since every yrkande seeks remand
  and it will be the agency's job to assess that on return; and background facts no ground depends
  on. In practice the load-bearing set is small — the decision, any föreläggande, the dagboksblad,
  and the documents behind the specific samhörighet facts pleaded under Ground B. A long exhibit
  list is not thoroughness; it buries the two or three documents that matter and asks the appellant
  to gather papers they don't need.
- **6 ÖVRIGA UPPLYSNINGAR** — oral hearing (usually not requested where everything is documentary);
  reservation to develop the case, adduce further evidence, adjust the yrkanden.
- Place, date, signature line, name.

## 8. Building the BAKGRUND

Section 4 may argue only from facts stated here with a source attached; an unsourced assertion
discredits the argument resting on it. A fact earns its place only if a section 4 paragraph relies
on it, the court needs it for admissibility, or it's materially adverse and will surface anyway.

**Coverage, chronological.** Group them so each numbered paragraph does one job.

- **Who the applicant is and how the residence began** — nationality, arrival, purpose of coming,
  folkbokföring, the succession of permits, PUT, household. Sourced from personbevis, the PUT
  decision, employment certificate.
- **Why the application satisfied the old law** — application date, and the residence computation
  against the then-current five-year requirement.
- **Integrity of the residence period** — absences abroad and whether they bear on the
  computation. State this even when the answer is that they don't; it's the first thing the agency
  probes, and silence reads as concealment.
- **The dröjsmål history** — the 12 § FL begäran, the refusal, the appeal, the föreläggande with
  målnummer and date. For A2, the plain duration from initiation to decision, plus any begäran that
  didn't reach a court.
- **What the file shows about handling** — recorded measures and, importantly, their absence; where
  the 6 June 2026 entry into force of 2026:447 without transitional provisions falls in the
  sequence.
- **The decision** — the ground it rests on, the exception sentence verbatim, and whether the
  remaining conditions were examined at all.
- **Service and timeliness** — last paragraph. ⚠ Show the computation: sent, kontrollmeddelande,
  deemed service, last day. Never "har uppfattat" the deadline, or filed "inom den tid som anges i
  beslutet" — that asserts timeliness instead of demonstrating it, and it's checked first.

**Three registers of certainty, kept distinct.** What a document affirmatively shows; what a
document does *not* show; what the applicant asserts from their own knowledge. Phrase each as what
it is. "Av dagboksbladet framgår inte att …" is a different and far safer claim than "Ingen åtgärd
vidtogs", and the negative-form version is usually all the argument needs.

**Two dates where there are two.** Where a date could be computed more than one way — arrival
versus folkbokföring — plead the one you rely on and the conservative fallback in the same breath.
A clause, and one line of attack removed.

**Register.** Facts, not characterisation. Chronology carries the implication — a decision taken
the day after the first recorded measure needs no adverb. Fault-and-motive words ("passivitet",
"underlåtenhet") belong in section 4 if anywhere; true sequence markers (*först*, elapsed periods)
are fine.

**Adverse facts.** Disclose material ones briefly, without defending them; answer them in section
4. ⚠ "Material" means bearing on a ground actually pleaded. The *absence* of something not relied
on — no dröjsmålstalan, no föreläggande, no family in Sweden, no association membership — is not an
adverse fact but a weakness volunteered. Say nothing about moves you aren't making, in section 3 or
4. No rule of candour requires cataloguing what the applicant lacks.
⚠ Nor may a fact be pleaded and then undercut in the same breath — "klaganden har goda kunskaper i
svenska, men något provresultat åberopas inte" invites the court to discount it. Either state the
fact plainly, or leave it out. Note that undocumented facts are still worth pleading: the appellant
states them, and §12's sweep removes only the hedging, not the fact.

**Anchoring and cross-reference.** Each paragraph carries its source — "(bilaga 3)", or a reference
to the decision itself. Number the paragraphs so section 4 can point back to them, and actually
point back: it lets the court move between the chronology and the argument without hunting.

⚠ Where the applicant hasn't supplied a date or document, leave a labelled blank. Do not
reconstruct a plausible chronology.

## 9. Argument map

Select; don't exhaust. A move that doesn't fit these facts weakens the ones that do.

**Two rules before you start.**

- **An authority never travels without its proposition.** Cite HFD 2021 ref. 12 punkt 24 for the
  *modification* — legal certainty, fairness, no dependence on chance — not for the baseline, which
  is RÅ 1988 ref. 132's. A citation attached to the wrong proposition, or to no proposition, reads
  as decoration and invites the court to check the rest.
- **Each group has a minimum.** Group A needs the baseline, detriment-plus-silence and the chance
  point (first four bullets below); without them it collapses into Group C wearing a different name.
  Group B needs the architecture and restrictive-isn't-never points. Group C needs 23 § and 32 §
  both. **If you can't state a group's minimum, don't plead that group** — say so rather than
  gesturing at it and inviting the court to construct it.

### A — 11 § applies in its pre-6 June 2026 wording

- Baseline: absent transitional provisions, motive statements or other circumstances, the rules in
  force when the examination takes place apply, to both procedure and substance — HFD 2021 ref. 12
  punkt 23, citing RÅ 1988 ref. 132, RÅ 1993 ref. 41 and RÅ 2003 ref. 81, and comparing NJA 2012
  s. 559. Open with this against yourself.
- Refined and modified in later case law — HFD 2021 ref. 12 punkt 24, citing RÅ 1996 ref. 57,
  RÅ 2000 ref. 11, RÅ 2003 ref. 88 and RÅ 2004 ref. 82, and comparing NJA 2020 s. 285. ⚠ Punkt 24
  states three considerations, and only two help: that the effects must satisfy reasonable demands
  of legal certainty and fairness, and that the choice of applicable rules should not depend on
  chance. The first — that the purpose of an amendment should be able to take effect — cuts the
  other way here. Quote the limbs you rely on; don't paraphrase punkt 24 as though all of it
  favoured the appellant, because the agency will read it out in full.
- ⚠ **Concede punkt 25 explicitly.** HFD went on to say that for changes to *substantive*
  provisions the principle often takes full effect where the question isn't regulated by
  transitional provisions, since every instance applies the same rules at its own examination. The
  hemvistkrav is a substantive provision, so this is the passage most directly against Ground A.
  The argument is not that punkt 25 is wrong but that punkt 24's chance limb bites on these facts —
  say that, rather than citing the case as if punkt 25 weren't in it.
- Detriment plus legislative silence: direct application disadvantaging the individual often should
  not occur unless prescribed transitionally or otherwise discernible as intended — NJA 2020 s. 285
  punkt 18.
- Chance and unequal treatment: problematic to apply a new rule where it was essentially chance —
  processing time in some instance — that entry into force passed during handling; like cases then
  treated differently by speed of decision — NJA 2020 s. 285 punkterna 19 och 24. The
  transition-less rule was not applied to proceedings commenced earlier. Lagrådet described the
  position the same way — prop. 2025/26:175 bilaga 11.
- Neutralise the counter: that citizenship is assessed on circumstances at the time of examination
  (MIG 2007:28) concerns which *facts* apply, not which *wording* of an amended provision. ⚠ Make
  this move whenever Ground A is pleaded; the agency will reach for MIG 2007:28 first.
- Concede, then narrow — **the hinge, and now a steeper one.** Transitional provisions were not
  merely omitted: the Government declined to propose them and gave reasons (prop. 2025/26:175
  avsnitt 14), and the riksdag rejected motions calling for them, over reservations from V and MP
  and from S, V, C and MP (bet. 2025/26:SfU28). ⚠ Concede all of this openly — the agency will
  produce it otherwise. Direct application to cases decided in the ordinary way was plainly
  intended. The surviving question is narrow: whether it was intended where the moment of decision
  was set by non-compliance with a court order (A1), or by failure to decide an application ready
  long before (A2). On that, the materials say nothing. Never inflate this into "direct application
  was never intended" — after SfU28 that claim is simply false and discredits the rest.
- The knowledge-requirement asymmetry: the only legal-certainty reasoning in the materials concerns
  kunskapskraven — acquire the knowledge and reapply, or be exempted for incapacity — prop.
  2025/26:175 avsnitt 14 s. 119 f. No counterpart for hemvist, and time of residence can't be
  affected by anything the applicant does.
- Equal treatment: objectivity, impartiality, equality before the law — 1 kap. 9 § RF, 5 § FL.
  Lagrådet's concern was precisely that like-cases-alike is undermined by long processing times in
  citizenship cases — prop. bilaga 11. Earlier amendments regularly let older provisions govern
  cases already commenced and decisions under appeal — transitional provisions to 2024:423 p. 3–4,
  2024:424 p. 2, 2005:722 p. 2 — and within this same reform such protection went to applicants
  relying on older knowledge tests (2026:449 p. 3) but not to this group.
- ⚠ **Expect NJA 2012 s. 559 in reply.** There, in the absence of transitional provisions, a new
  miljöbalken provision was applied to cases initiated before entry into force — and HFD cites it in
  punkt 23 as part of the baseline. Distinguish rather than ignore: it concerned a resning
  application and a rule conferring a municipal veto protecting third-party interests, not a
  requirement operating to the detriment of an individual applicant whose application was already
  complete. NJA 2020 s. 285, decided later on a transition-less rule that *was* to the individual's
  detriment, is the closer analogue.
- **[A2 especially]** The counterfactual, made concrete: had the case been decided within a
  reasonable time, the earlier wording would have applied. Anchor it to Migrationsverket's own
  published handläggningstider for citizenship cases at the relevant period — that converts "there
  was delay" into a measured comparison against the agency's own benchmark. Back it with 9 § FL,
  under which a case shall be handled as simply and quickly as possible without sacrificing legal
  certainty.
- Attribute the causation: the delay was not caused by any act or omission of the applicant. ⚠
  Anchor it — no komplettering was requested, nothing was outstanding — rather than asserting it as
  a conclusion.
- Optional, modest: 2 kap. 10 § RF expresses Swedish law's resistance to retroactive burdens.
  ⚠ It covers only criminal law and taxes and charges, so concede that in the same sentence and use
  it as the expression of a value, not as a governing rule. Without the concession it looks like
  overreach.
- Close on what determined the choice of law *in this case*, then ask for remand for examination
  under the old wording, noting the application met those requirements when filed.

### B — Särskilda skäl under 11 c §

- Architecture: 11 c § första stycket permits naturalisation despite unmet 11 § requirements where
  there are särskilda skäl; st. 2–4 restrict this for certain requirements only, and **not for
  hemvist**; the two formerly enumerated grounds have left the provision and reappear in 11 a § as
  shorter residence periods — prop. 2025/26:175 avsnitt 11.1. Backbone of Ground B.
- Not theoretical: dispensation available from all conditions in 11 § — prop. 1999/2000:147 s. 49 f.
  via MIG 2007:28; granted in practice from hemvist on the general ground — MIG 2015:7; the
  Government notes dispensation has been given chiefly from the principal residence requirement and
  that those groups may still be exempted — prop. avsnitt 11.1.
- Restrictive ≠ never: safety valve, applied very restrictively to avoid manifestly unreasonable
  results, retained for unreasonable consequences or conflict with convention obligations — prop.
  avsnitt 11.1 (referring to prop. 1950:217 s. 37). Rarity describes how often the conditions are
  met, not a cap. ⚠ Pair with an express disclaimer of any unconditional right — there is none even
  where the conditions are met, naturalisation presupposing a discretionary assessment
  (prop. 1975/76:136 s. 34; MIG 2007:28,
  MIG 2017:7).
- Purpose turned around: the residence requirement is set at the time generally needed for
  foreigners to become part of Swedish society, and should not be set higher — prop. 1975/76:136
  s. 28 f. (the departementspromemoria's formulation at s. 28, adopted by the statsråd at s. 29).
  MIG 2017:7 states the same purpose in terms of knowing Swedish conditions and belonging to
  Swedish society. Where that purpose is already served on the facts, an exception is consistent
  with the scheme rather than a departure from it.
- The travaux expressly contemplate individual relaxation: beyond the enumerated dispensation
  grounds, in individual cases there may be reason to allow certain relaxations of the residence
  requirement having regard to humanitarian, social or personal circumstances — prop. 1975/76:136
  s. 30 f. (guidelines at s. 30, endorsed by the statsråd as well founded at s. 31); reproduced in
  MIG 2015:7. ⚠ Note the wording is *lättnader beträffande hemvistkravet* specifically, not a
  general dispensation formula — which is why it fits Ground B better than it looks. Beyond the statutory
  criteria the authority may weigh other circumstances bearing on samhörighet, and in MIG 2017:7
  training and employment counted in the applicant's favour. Where the purpose is already served,
  exception is consistent with the scheme rather than a departure from it.
- The stated rationale doesn't reach this case: the security interest is given as the principal
  material reason for the tightening, weighing very heavily — prop. avsnitt 7.1. Where neither
  security nor criminality arises, say so once, plainly.
- **[A1]** What distinguishes this applicant from everyone whose case merely wasn't reached:
  typically that the application met the then-current requirements when filed and the refusal rested
  solely on residence; that it was ready for decision with no supplementation ever requested; that a
  court ordered *this* case decided snarast; and that the order went unanswered until after entry
  into force. The unmet requirement is a direct consequence. ⚠ Assert only what an exhibit supports.
- **[A1]** Integrity of the remedy: requesting a decision exists to strengthen legal certainty
  where cases drag on unreasonably — prop. 2016/17:180 s. 114 f. If failure to comply with a 49 §
  order costs the individual a substantive position held when it was made, the remedy is hollowed
  out. JO has seriously criticised Migrationsverket for passivity after such orders, finding many
  court decisions in practice not followed — beslut 2025-09-23 dnr 7753-2024; JK likewise —
  dnr 2022/4747, 2024/6800, 2024/7745. A widespread failing isn't one the individual should bear.
- Reasonable time: applications shall be processed within a reasonable time — art. 10 ECN
  (ETS 166; SÖ 2001:20); the state shouldn't profit from its own delay. The Government retained the
  exception partly for situations where refusal would conflict with convention obligations — prop.
  avsnitt 11.1 — so this is the provision's declared function, not an import. One paragraph.
- Samhörighet — factual, and the applicant's: residence, work, family, home, associations, elected
  or voluntary positions, study, language, civic participation. Each proposition needs a document.
  Where evidence isn't gathered yet: say the account isn't exhaustive, note that no inquiry into
  personal circumstances was obtained, offer further material on the court's request within the time
  it sets. ⚠ Don't generate these facts. Ask, or leave blanks.
- Meet the adverse authority: establishment alone isn't sufficient and a need for citizenship isn't
  särskilda skäl — MIG 2017:7, where factors against prevailed despite training and employment.
  State it before it's stated against you, then address whether anything in *this* file weighs
  against. If something does, deal with it.

### C — The exception question was neither investigated nor examined

- Why the applicant said nothing about it: the application met every condition as 11 § then read, so
  no exception question arose and there was no reason to invoke circumstances supporting one. After
  the amendment the outcome turned on 11 c §, decided adversely without notice or any opportunity to
  put forward relevant circumstances.
- Duty to investigate: the case must be investigated as its nature requires, and the authority must
  where necessary work towards the party clarifying or supplementing — 23 § FL. The party's own
  responsibility concerns material the party wishes to invoke; here the submission met the law as it
  stood, so there was nothing to supplement. Where the legal basis changes mid-handling and the
  outcome turns on a question the applicant had no reason to address, the authority must act. The
  11 c § assessment turns on individual circumstances, and that material can come essentially only
  from the applicant.
- Reasons: quote the agency's exception sentence exactly; the decision identifies no circumstance
  taken into account; a statement that doesn't show an assessment was made isn't an assessment that
  came out adversely. **[A1]** it also passes over the circumstance most obviously calling for a
  position — the court order and the change of law before compliance — though the order sits in the
  agency's own diary. Fails the requirement of a clarifying statement of reasons identifying the
  decisive circumstances — 32 § FL; cf. art. 11 ECN (written reasons).
- Optional, fact-dependent: 25 § FL requires material added to the case by someone other than the
  party to be communicated before a decision goes against them. ⚠ Only plead this where the diary
  actually shows late-added material — register checks run shortly before the decision, say. The
  change of law is not "material added", so on most records this does not fit.
- Why a yttrande can't cure it: what's missing is the applicant's material, never obtained, not
  the agency's reasons — and the remaining conditions were never examined. First-instance assessment
  in court would proceed on no record prepared for the purpose and be reviewable only on leave to
  appeal. Whether the court can examine as first instance depends on the case, and it may indicate
  how the inquiry should be supplemented — prop. avsnitt 14 s. 119 (citing MIG 2007:31); 8 § FPL.
  Lagrådet assumed remand on the instansordningsprincip — prop. bilaga 11; Migrationsöverdomstolen
  has done so in a citizenship case — MIG 2007:28.

### D — International law (optional, one paragraph, any ground)

- Acquisition of nationality falls within the ambit of art. 8 ECHR, opening art. 14 — *Genovese v.
  Malta*, no. 53124/09 (11 Oct 2011). "Other status" extends beyond innate characteristics to
  status flowing from legal circumstances — *Clift v. UK*, no. 7205/07 (13 July 2010) §§ 56–58; cf.
  *Bah v. UK*, no. 56328/07 (27 Sept 2011) §§ 45–46. The difference between this applicant and one
  decided before entry into force lies in the agency's pace, not their own circumstances.
  Nationality competence is exercised with due regard to Union law — *Micheletti*, C-369/90 p. 10.
- ⚠ Frame as convention-conforming interpretation of 11 §, and state expressly that the provision as
  such isn't contended incompatible and that no review under 11 kap. 14 § RF is requested. Otherwise
  it becomes a constitutional challenge the court will decline.

### Supporting, not load-bearing

These come up in circulating source lists. None of them wins a case on its own, and the refusals
being issued are not turning on whether an appellant cited them — so including one is not the
disaster it might look like. The real risk is displacement: space and emphasis spent here is space
not spent on Groups A to C, which is where these cases are actually decided.

- **EU law — the Charter (arts. 7, 41, 47) and the CJEU nationality line** (Rottmann, Tjebbes, JY,
  *Kommissionen mot Malta*). Scope is the difficulty: the Charter binds member states when they
  implement EU law (art. 51.1), and those judgments concern *loss* of citizenship entailing loss of
  Union citizenship, so a third-country national's naturalisation in Sweden sits outside the
  clearest part of that case law. If the appellant wants it in, keep it to a short passage, put it
  after the Swedish grounds, and frame it as support rather than as a self-standing ground —
  *Micheletti* punkt 10 (due regard to Union law) is the safest anchor. Where the appellant is
  themselves a Union citizen, or citizenship status has EU consequences on the facts, the argument
  is stronger and worth developing properly.
- **ECHR art. 6** — generally held inapplicable to nationality and immigration proceedings, so
  don't build a "fair trial within reasonable time" argument on it. Art. 10 ECN carries the
  reasonable-time point instead, and carries it better.
- **Berättigade förväntningar** — weak in Swedish law against legislative change. It can colour
  Group A; it shouldn't be pleaded as a ground in its own right.
- **2 kap. 11 § RF** — governs *rättegång*, not agency handling. Wrong provision for this
  complaint.

## 10. Style

- Formal klarspråk. Short sentences. No anglicisms, no rhetorical questions, no adjectives doing
  argumentative work. Understatement with a pinpoint attached is the strongest register in this
  forum.
- ⚠ **Plain text, not markdown.** This file is written in markdown; the filed document is not. No
  `#` headings, no `**bold**`, no bullet lists anywhere in it. Section headings are plain lines
  ("1 YRKANDEN", "4.2 Utrednings- och motiveringsfrågan"); everything under them is numbered
  paragraphs in continuous prose. Where a move calls for an enumeration — the Ground A1 circumstances,
  say — run it as prose ("För det första… För det andra…"), not as a list.
- Citation forms — statutes: "11 § första stycket medborgarskapslagen", "lagen (2001:82) om svenskt
  medborgarskap". Travaux: "prop. 2025/26:175 avsnitt 11.1", then "a. prop. s. 119 f.". Cases:
  "MIG 2007:28", "HFD 2021 ref. 12, punkt 24", "NJA 2020 s. 285, punkterna 19 och 24". Signals:
  "se", "jfr", "se bl.a.", "samma rättsfall".
- Dates in full: "den 15 juli 2026", never 2026-07-15 in running text.
- "Klaganden" throughout; "Migrationsverket", not "verket", on first mention in a section. Recast
  rather than guess a pronoun where the applicant's details are unknown.
- Rule before application, per subsection. Cross-reference internally — "(avsnitt 3.2)".
- Length follows the record. Padding a thin case to the length of a strong one signals the weakness.

**Register calibration.** Six sentence shapes worth being able to produce. Write your own
equivalents rather than lifting these — the point is the move each one makes.

- Conceding, then turning: *Såvitt avser ärenden som avgörs i vanlig ordning kan en direkt
  tillämpning sägas vara åsyftad. Frågan i målet är emellertid en annan.*
- A negative stated in provable form: *Av dagboksbladet framgår inte att någon handläggningsåtgärd
  vidtogs mellan den [X] och den [Y].*
- Understatement carrying the argument: *En restriktiv tillämpning är emellertid inte detsamma som
  en utebliven tillämpning.*
- Disclaiming the overreach before it's imputed: *Klaganden gör inte gällande någon ovillkorlig rätt
  till medborgarskap.*
- Characterising the decision without editorialising: *Beslutet anger inte någon omständighet som
  har beaktats i den frågan.*
- Offering rather than demanding: *Ytterligare utredning ges in på domstolens begäran och inom den
  tid som domstolen bestämmer.*

## 12. Before filing, and what to tell the user

**Two mechanical passes, run over the finished draft before anything else.** Both catch failures
that principles alone have not.

1. **The negative-inventory sweep.** Read every sentence and delete any that describes what the
   appellant lacks, hasn't got, or isn't relying on. Search for *inte har*, *saknas*, *saknar*,
   *åberopar inte*, *det finns inte något*, *inte något intyg*, *ingen*, *inga*. A sentence saying
   there is no family in Sweden, no association activity, no test certificate, no dröjsmålstalan,
   no föreläggande, or that a fact is invoked "med viss försiktighet" because it can't be
   documented, goes out — the whole sentence, not a softened version. Two exceptions survive: a
   disclaimer of a legal position not being advanced (*klaganden gör inte gällande någon
   ovillkorlig rätt*), and an adverse fact that bears on a ground actually pleaded. Everything else
   is a weakness volunteered, and nothing requires it. If a tie can't be documented, either plead
   it plainly as a fact or leave it out; don't plead it and then undercut it.
2. **The unseen-document sweep.** Search for *dagboksbladet*, *aktbilaga*, and any other document
   the draft characterises. For each, confirm the appellant actually holds it. If not, delete every
   characterisation of its contents and keep only the request that it be produced. ⚠ No draft may
   both describe a document's contents and ask the court to obtain it.

**Then check.** Yrkanden and section 4 mapped against each other — every yrkande argued, every argument
asked for, none conditioned on the court accepting the appellant's own ground. Every citation opened
— existence, holding, pinpoint — and attached to the proposition it actually supports; watch the
traps (NJA paragraph numbering; prop pages shifting from the lagrådsremiss; old 12 § versus new
11 c §; aktbilaga numbers matching the actual diary). Deadline recomputed and shown. The decision's
exception sentence quoted character for character. Every absolute — "enda", "aldrig", "alltid",
"endast", "samtliga" — literally true against a document in the file. No bracketed placeholder
surviving, and none that asked the user to supply an argument. Plain text throughout, no markdown.
Exhibits numbered, each with a bevistema, each attached. Signed, dated, dossiernummer and contact
details present. Filed with Migrationsverket, addressed to the court the decision names, in time.

**Tell the user,** briefly and without alarm: the deadline as a date, and to file early rather
than perfect; which grounds the draft rests on and an honest word on their strength; that the
central question is contested and the legislative history is substantially against them, so a
well-built appeal can still fail; that a further appeal to Migrationsöverdomstolen requires
prövningstillstånd; the blanks still to fill and documents needed as exhibits; that every citation
should be opened and checked; that this is a draft needing real adaptation, not a finished appeal,
and what they file is their responsibility; that an incomplete appeal filed in time can be
developed later while a late one can't be filed at all; and that a Swedish migration lawyer should
review it, since this is not legal advice.

⚠ **And say so if the draft came out thin.** If you could only plead one group, or had to drop
authorities you couldn't state confidently, tell the user in plain terms — that the appeal is
structurally sound but light on authority, that this is a limit of what you could do rather than a
reading of their case, and that here an hour of a lawyer's time is the difference between filing
and filing well. A thin draft filed in time still has value; a thin draft the user believes is
strong does not.

**End with the practical list.** Easy to overlook, and each one can sink an otherwise sound filing:

- Fill in every English-labelled blank — search the document for `[` and confirm none remain.
- Attach every exhibit listed in section 5, and remove from the list anything not attached.
- Check the factual statements against your own documents; correct anything that isn't right.
- Sign and date it, and check the place and date at the top match.
- Confirm the dossiernummer and your contact details are present and correct.
- Send it to Migrationsverket, addressed to the court named in the decision, before the deadline —
  and keep proof of when you sent it.
- Optional but worth it if you can: have a Swedish migration lawyer read it first.
